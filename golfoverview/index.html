2026 Masters Range Analysis

Link to the page

Major tournaments aren’t hosted on the Tourcast website although I assume they are captured by the same companies. The dedicated Master’s website had a live visualisation capturing players’ shots on the range. I had to create another script to Tourcast but the data is very similar expect the shots have actual 3D positions at timestamps instead of polynomials.

The motivation was to answer the question of if shot intent can be ascertained from a range session and therefore player dispersion by clustering shots. The simple metric created, that can only worsen with additional shots (players making the cut are going to hit a lot more shots at the range), correlated with finish position. So to some extent it can.

Inspiration for the presentation from this amazing machine learning visualisation created by r2d3.

Shot by shot model

As a betting man the natural thing to make from the data is a prediction model and with the granularity of the data a shot by shot model is the logical and by far the most interesting route.

There are quite a few papers that use shot by shot models. Most famously is Strokes Gained concept by Broadie. Subsequent papers have added complexity but the features created have been fairly basic and naive. With the geometry of the greens and holes I believe more insightful features can be created.

This Data Golf visualisation is an example of what I want the shot by shot model to be able to solve before the fact. I also want it to be able to make different decisions based on the position of the pin.

Because of this the plan is to start from the pin/putting and work backwards to the tee. Putting is also the easiest part of the golf game to model with additional variables like wind, spin, lie, etc etc able to be ignored.

Interesting greens/putts:

  • TPC San Antonio (Oaks) - Hole 16

    • A doughnut green showing off the layup logic

  • TPC Craig Ranch - Hole 6

    • One of the most interesting greens/pins. Especially when putting over the fringe isn’t an option. Very happy that the algorithm was able to find this layup.

  • Puntacana (Corales) - Hole 12

    • A two tiered green that shows the value of being on the correct tier very cleanly. The ball can easily end up back at your feet or way past the hole with the round one pin. You’d rather it be further away with the like in R2/4 when putting from the bottom. The margin for error is tiny when putting from the top on tour speed greens with the round 3 pin. These could potentially be solved with a much finer search matrix.

  • The Riviera - Hole 8

    • Another doughnut hole but with the fastest greens of the year and no way to find I decent putt. This would force players to chip if caught on the wrong side of the green.

  • TPC Sawgrass - Hole 17

    • Tiger’s famous putt on the island green in 2001.

Inspiration/References

  • Penner, AR (2002), The physics of putting

    • Inspiration for the solving of each putt and creation of many of the features

    • Adopted hole capture model and friction constants

      • Was also able to validate them against actual tour putts

    • Makes the surprising claim that “for a 10 ft putt the probability of making the downhill putt is approximately 2.9 times greater than the probability of making the uphill putt”

      • Managed to reach similar results in that the size of the capture window (the range of initial ball speeds and launch angles that result in the ball being holed) is larger for downhill putts, however, inclusion in the 1 putt model didn’t increase its predictive performance

  • Gelman, A. and D. Nolan (2002). A probability model for golf putting

    • Essentially a distance only putting model used as a baseline

  • Drappi, C. and L. Co Ting Keh (2019). Predicting golf scores at the shot level

    • Adopted their 1/3 putt models with 2 putts the remaining probability. 4 putts and above have zero probability

Found these afterwards but other cool ways the green geometry was used:

  • Stöckl, M., et al. (2012). Analysing individual performance in golf using the ISOPAR Method

    • Spatial putting model creates a spline of the surface

    • Competitor to Strokes Gained

  • Yousefi/Swartz 2013

    • Bayesian spatial map

Short Game (within 50m? of the green)

  • Can different shot types (bump and run, flop etc) can clustered/classified from the height/spin data?

    • Could also use if the player is shot sided or has a big height differential between the ball and the hole

    • Biggest issue with this is that very slight differences in the lie (which isn’t captured in the data) will change player decision making

  • The putting model expected putts can be used as the end state variable

    • Small worry with bringing biases of the putting model into this model

      • On oddly shaped greens the model has no concept of the fringe or putting over short fairways

  • Feature ideas

    • Distance to the hole

    • Lie (sand, rough, fairway etc)

      • Broadie found that sand is easier than rough only 15-34 yards from the hole so each lie needs its own fit to distance

    • Short-sided

      • How close does the pin need to be to the edge of the green?

    • Elevation change relative to distance

    • Green slope

      • Example from Data Golf - if the green slope funnels the ball towards the hole chipping will be easier

    • Wind will have slightly more impact than putting

    • Commentators often note chipping into the wind can help with getting the ball up and landing it softly on the green

    • Random effects for player and tournament

      • Courses alter rough etc from tournament to tournament

      • Weather also changes conditions - Rain on the day could also be a feature

Approach shots

  • Plan to create a shot dispersion map for each player that creates a distribution of outcomes on the previous models for a chosen target

    • Determining intention will be the challenge here to create the player/lie profiles

Computer Vision Putting

I wanted to answer the question asked every Master’s by commentators “if an amateur started on the green could they beat the pros from their normal tees”. This was before I realised that the Master’s had a different website without detailed scans of the green but it can be done with most of the courses on the tour. The sim has also been gamified to play against the 2026 Pebble Beach field or locally against friends.

Used Python opencv and a 60fps camera to identify a ball on my putting matt and the orientation with the lines on it. Recorded the direction and speed of the putt to the putting simulation via a websocket.

The ball recognition was done by following this blog post. I had to use a yellow ball (at least I found some use for them) to properly differentiate from the white lines on the putting mat.
